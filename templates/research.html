RESEARCH FOUNDATION

Research Papers

This project explores methods for detecting AI-generated text using deep learning, Transformer models and statistical approaches. The following research papers provide the academic foundation for the project.

PAPER 01 • 2023

DetectGPT: Zero-Shot Machine-Generated Text Detection using Probability Curvature

Authors: Eric Mitchell, Yoonho Lee, Alexander Khazatsky, Christopher D. Manning and Chelsea Finn

DetectGPT proposes a zero-shot method for identifying machine-generated text by examining the curvature of a language model's log probability function. The approach does not require training a separate classifier.

LLM Detection Zero-Shot Probability Machine-Generated Text
PAPER 02 • 2023

Fast-DetectGPT: Efficient Zero-Shot Detection of Machine-Generated Text via Conditional Probability Curvature

Authors: Guangsheng Bao, Yanbin Zhao, Zhiyang Teng, Linyi Yang and Yue Zhang

Fast-DetectGPT improves the efficiency of probability-curvature-based detection. The paper introduces conditional probability curvature and evaluates the method across multiple datasets and source language models.

Deep Learning Zero-Shot Probability Curvature Text Detection
PAPER 03 • 2024

Spotting LLMs With Binoculars: Zero-Shot Detection of Machine-Generated Text

Authors: Abhimanyu Hans, Avi Schwarzschild, Valeriia Cherepanova, Hamid Kazemi, Aniruddha Saha, Micah Goldblum, Jonas Geiping and Tom Goldstein

Binoculars introduces a detection method based on contrasting the outputs of two related language models. The method is designed to detect machine-generated text without requiring training data for the detector.

LLMs Zero-Shot Machine Text Language Models

Connection to Our Project

The research papers demonstrate different approaches to AI-generated text detection. Our project focuses on a Transformer-based binary classification approach using a fine-tuned RoBERTa model to classify text as likely human-written or AI-generated.


The project demonstrates how Natural Language Processing, deep learning and Transformer architectures can be applied to the problem of AI-generated text detection.

RoBERTa Foundation

RoBERTa is a robustly optimized version of BERT developed for language representation learning. Its Transformer architecture makes it suitable for downstream NLP classification tasks.
